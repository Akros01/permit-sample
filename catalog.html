Which one to buy

The reports are not interchangeable. Pick by the work you sell.

Pools and pool service

The biggest pool lists are Austin (175 pool addresses), Dallas (183) and Los Angeles (142). Austin is the one for competitive work: 175 addresses from 85 different contractors. Collin County adds 50 from 33 firms.

HVAC, electrical, plumbing

Dallas is densest: 950 plumbing, 440 electrical, 498 HVAC change-outs, 206 water heaters. Austin next. Sacramento is small but almost pure replacement work.

Roofing

Los Angeles is the outlier at 1,184 re-roofs in under a month. Chicago 467, Dallas 265, Sacramento 117, Collin 106. Austin publishes few (41) — not the one for roofs.

New construction

Austin leads with 502 new houses, then Collin County 293, Dallas 238 and Chicago 139.

The trade work that follows is uneven: Dallas 136 further permits at its new-house addresses, Austin 167, Los Angeles 11, Chicago 5.

Foundations and drainage

Collin County is the only report with a real foundation cohort — 140 permits, driven by new-build settling.

Fire alarms and life safety

Chicago is the only feed here that publishes alarms as their own permits — 103 in the window, covering installs, repairs and modifications. A keyword scan of the other five finds only incidental mentions — irrigation sprinklers, alarms mentioned inside a larger job — so their absence from those reports is a gap in the source, not in the work.

Fences

Dallas 163, Chicago 71, Collin County 31, Austin 3. Dallas files fences as residential accessory-structure permits, which is why they never surfaced as a trade; Austin describes them plainly inside building permits, so it yields a short list; Los Angeles and Sacramento fold fencing into broader scopes and produce none.

ZIP-level targeting

All six carry ZIP codes. Five publish them on the permit itself; Chicago's feed has no property ZIP, so each row's ZIP is worked out from the parcel coordinates the city publishes, placed inside the city's own ZIP boundary map. That resolves 2,436 of 2,476 rows (98%) across 59 ZIP codes; the rest carry a blank ZIP rather than a guess.

What every download contains

report.html

The report itself. Opens in any browser, prints cleanly to PDF.

permits.csv

One row per permit: date, permit number, job type, address, ZIP, description, permit type, contractor and value where published, and a link to that permit's record. In Austin the link opens the city's permit page. The other five cities publish no per-permit page, so there the link opens the city's own JSON record: the same data as the row, best read in a JSON viewer or imported straight into a spreadsheet. Same columns in every metro, so the six combine into one spreadsheet.

SOURCES.txt

The source dataset or ArcGIS layer, the exact filter used, and the retrieval time.

Where each one is weak

Stated here so nobody buys the wrong report.

  • Austin — the city publishes a job value on only 20 of 3,968 permits, so the dollar column is effectively empty. Few re-roofs. 18 of its permits are supplemental revisions — resubmittals that change plans on permits already issued, not new work.
  • Los Angeles — no contractor column. 19% of its permits are supplemental revisions to permits already issued — filed to change plans, not new work — so count the job types, not the row totals. 0.4% remain Other.
  • Collin County — value on 29% of rows; the smallest Texas list at 879 permits.
  • Dallas — no dollar values at all and no contractor column. Buy it for volume and mix.
  • Chicago — ZIPs derived from coordinates rather than published (98% of rows), and only 4 pool addresses. 9% are supplemental revisions — contractor swaps, plan amendments, changes of owner — so count the trades; 0.8% remain Other.
  • Sacramento — smallest list at 635 and its feed runs about two weeks behind; coverage ends 25 October. 11 pool addresses.
